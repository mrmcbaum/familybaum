Why rebuild their DNA?

The more complete a DNA reconstruction is, the more DNA matches we can identify, and the better we can see exactly how each of them is related.

Imagine a 10,000-piece jigsaw puzzle where every piece is a genetic match. Each piece has its place. As Gaylord, Arda and their ancestors come back into focus, we can see where every match fits into the whole picture. That is how we solve family tree mysteries and break through genealogical brick walls.

Most grandparents have no bar yet: their DNA is still mixed inside their children's kits, or no cousins from their other children have tested yet. Each new tester on those lines starts filling them in.

Updated 27 November 2026: two new testers from Dott's sister Phebe's family added about 62 cM of Dott's DNA. The older generations are rebuilt from Gaylord's profile plus matches who descend from those couples, so every new tester on those lines makes their bars grow.

Three ways to take part

Any one of these helps. Your results stay private to the project: we never publish living people's DNA or names, and you can withdraw at any time.

1. Update this site

Send photos, documents, stories and family updates. We'll add them to the right family page.

  • Photos & documents: photos@familybaum.com
  • Stories & information: info@familybaum.com

2. Share your DNA results

Already have a DNA test? Add Michael Baum (michael@familybaum.com) as a Manager or Collaborator on your existing profile at Ancestry, MyHeritage, FamilyTreeDNA, 23andMe or elsewhere. You stay the owner and can remove him at any time.

Step-by-step guide (PDF)

3. Get tested

Join the project with a DNA test. Either buy one (AncestryDNA preferred) or email michael@familybaum.com and we'll send you a pre-registered test. Spit into the tube, mail it off, and we'll update you on the results!
